**single enterprise retail/sales dataset** that can support several demo narratives rather than creating separate datasets for each dashboard.

The goal is that the same data can demonstrate:

**Executive KPI → variance → hierarchy drill-down → anomaly → root cause → customer/product/supplier analysis → forecast → scenario → AI investigation.**

# 1. Demo dataset concept

### Dataset name

**Enterprise Sales & Profitability 360**

### Business

A fictional global technology retailer/manufacturer called **ApexTech**.

### Data period

**January 2024 – December 2026**

Use 2026 as the current/incomplete year so you can demonstrate:

* YTD
* PYTD
* YoY
* MoM
* QoQ
* forecast
* budget variance
* run-rate
* LTM
* seasonality

### Target size

Approximately **2–4 million sales order lines**.

That is large enough to make an enterprise analytics demo feel credible without making development painful.

# 19. Plant these 10 business events

This is the most important part of the entire specification.

## Event 1 — Supplier cost shock

**April 2026**

Supplier group:

> Global Components

increases component costs by **12%**.

Impact:

```text
COGS       +9–12%
Margin     -3–4 pts
Profit     ↓
```

But don't reduce sales.

This creates:

> **Revenue is growing while profit is declining.**

---

## Event 2 — Major promotion

**May–June 2026**

Electronics promotion.

Effect:

```text
Units       +22%
Revenue     +14%
Discount    +18%
Margin      -5 pts
```

This produces a classic:

> "Revenue growth isn't translating into profit."

story.

---

## Event 3 — New product launch

**May 2026**

Launch:

> MacBook Air M3

Generate:

```text
M3 sales       +300%
M2 sales        -28%
Mac category    +4%
```

This enables:

> **"Is M3 generating incremental demand or cannibalizing M2?"**

---

## Event 4 — Europe profitability problem

**June–August 2026**

Europe:

```text
Revenue       +8%
Units         +5%
Profit       -14%
```

Specifically:

```text
Germany
  ↓
Electronics
  ↓
Laptops
  ↓
Mac
```

Make this the largest contributor to the global profit decline.

---

## Event 5 — Strategic customer contraction

Customer:

> GlobalTech Corp

represents ~4% of company revenue.

Starting July:

```text
Orders       -25%
Revenue      -22%
```

Reason:

Contract renegotiation.

Now ask:

> "What is the revenue impact if our largest customer reduces purchases?"

---

## Event 6 — Inventory shortage

**July 2026**

A popular laptop SKU experiences:

```text
Demand       +30%
Inventory    -45%
Stockouts    +300%
```

Result:

Sales growth underperforms despite high demand.

This gives you:

> **"Are we losing sales because customers don't want the product, or because we don't have inventory?"**

---

## Event 7 — Product quality problem

One supplier begins producing defective components.

**August 2026**

For affected products:

```text
Return rate       3% → 11%
Defect rate       2% → 9%
Customer rating   ↓
Profit            ↓
```

Now you can connect:

**Supplier → Product → Returns → Customer → Profit**

---

## Event 8 — Sales anomaly

Create one unusually large enterprise order.

Normal:

```text
Customer monthly sales = $100K–$300K
```

August:

```text
$2.8M
```

Cause:

> One-time enterprise deployment.

This creates an obvious anomaly that AI can explain.

---

## Event 9 — Forecast deterioration

Starting September 2026:

Latest forecast predicts:

```text
Revenue shortfall = $4.2M
Profit shortfall  = $6.8M
```

Drivers:

```text
Europe margin
Supplier costs
Customer contraction
Inventory shortage
```

This gives you the executive forecasting story.

---

## Event 10 — Recovery scenario

Starting October:

Management actions:

```text
Supplier renegotiation       -7% cost
Promotion reduction          -4 pts discount
Inventory replenishment      +20% availability
Customer recovery             +10%
```

Forecast:

```text
Profit recovery = +$5.1M
```

Now you can demonstrate **what-if analysis**.

# 22. The killer demo flow

If I were presenting this to an executive, I would **not** show all 30 questions.

I'd tell one story.

### Step 1 — Executive overview

> "Revenue is growing 8.4%, but profit is down 5.7%."

---

### Step 2 — Ask AI

> **"Why is profit declining despite revenue growth?"**

AI identifies:

```text
Europe
Supplier costs
Discounting
Returns
Customer contraction
```

---

### Step 3 — Drill into Europe

Expand:

```text
Europe
 └── Germany
      └── Electronics
           └── Laptops
```

---

### Step 4 — Identify supplier problem

> **"Which suppliers are contributing to the margin decline?"**

AI finds:

> Global Components → +12% cost → $4.7M impact.

---

### Step 5 — Check inventory

> **"Are we also losing sales because of inventory shortages?"**

AI identifies the affected laptop SKUs.

---

### Step 6 — Investigate customer

> **"Which customers are contributing to the decline?"**

AI identifies GlobalTech Corp.

---

### Step 7 — Forecast

> **"What happens if nothing changes?"**

Forecast:

**$6.8M profit shortfall.**

---

### Step 8 — Scenario

Change:

```text
Supplier cost     -7%
Discount           -3 pts
Inventory          +20%
Customer recovery +10%
```

Result:

**Profit shortfall reduced from $6.8M → $1.7M.**

---

### Step 9 — Executive conclusion

The dashboard has gone from:

**"What happened?"**

to

**"Why did it happen?"**

to

**"What happens next?"**

to

**"What should we do?"**

That is the kind of demo story I'd optimize the synthetic data for.

# 23. One additional thing I'd strongly recommend

Don't make the synthetic data generator just produce random distributions.

Build it as a **scenario-driven generator**:

```text
                 ┌─────────────────────┐
                 │ Demo Scenario Config │
                 └──────────┬──────────┘
                            │
       ┌────────────────────┼────────────────────┐
       ▼                    ▼                    ▼
  Supplier Shock       Promotion             Customer
  +12% COGS            +22% Volume            -25%
       │                    │                    │
       └────────────────────┼────────────────────┘
                            ▼
                    Synthetic Generator
                            │
        ┌───────────────────┼───────────────────┐
        ▼                   ▼                   ▼
      Facts              Dimensions          Events
        │                   │                   │
        └───────────────────┼───────────────────┘
                            ▼
                  Enterprise Demo Dataset
```

That way you can later say:

> **"Generate me a pessimistic scenario where revenue grows 5%, but profit declines 12%."**

and regenerate the entire dataset while preserving referential integrity and the underlying business logic.

**That would make this much more valuable than a one-off CSV generator.** It becomes a reusable synthetic-data engine for demos.
