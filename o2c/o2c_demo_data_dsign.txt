**O2C is an excellent synthetic-data demo domain** because you can show a single customer/order moving through the entire commercial lifecycle and 
then demonstrate cross-functional analytics: sales, fulfillment, logistics, billing, cash, exceptions, and customer profitability.

For a compelling dashboard demo, create a connected **O2C data model with a common Order/Customer/Product spine**, plus deliberately planted business problems.

## 1. The demo story 

Your dashboard should let you tell a story like:

> **"We booked $128M of orders, but only $103M has been converted to cash. Where is the leakage?"**

Then drill through:

**Quote → Order → Fulfillment → Shipment → Invoice → Payment**

and uncover things like:

* Quotes with unusually low conversion
* Orders stuck in fulfillment
* Partial shipments
* Late deliveries
* Orders delivered but not invoiced
* Invoices overdue
* Customers paying slowly
* Credit-limit violations
* Price/margin leakage
* Disputed invoices
* Revenue trapped in the O2C cycle
* Customers with high sales but poor cash performance

That creates a much stronger demo than simply showing "sales by month."

# 3. The most important design decision: common IDs

The whole demo depends on being able to follow an O2C transaction.

For example:

```text
Quote
 Q-100245
     ↓
Order
 SO-204891
     ↓
Fulfillment
 FO-301245
     ↓
Shipment
 SH-501234
     ↓
Invoice
 INV-701245
     ↓
Payment
 PAY-901245
```

Use these relationships:

```text
customer_id
    │
    ├── quote
    │      │
    │      └── quote_line
    │
    └── sales_order
           │
           ├── order_line
           │
           ├── fulfillment
           │       └── shipment
           │
           └── invoice
                   │
                   ├── payment
                   └── dispute
```

This is what makes the data perfect for an Incorta demo.

# 13. Plant 10–15 deliberate business events

**This is extremely important.**

Don't make the synthetic data completely random.

Create specific problems that your dashboard can discover.

### Event 1 — Quote conversion collapse

One sales region:

```text
Quote conversion: 42% → 24%
```

### Event 2 — Discount explosion

A sales rep begins offering:

```text
Average discount: 8% → 22%
```

while sales volume rises only slightly.

### Event 3 — Warehouse bottleneck

One warehouse develops:

```text
Backorder rate: 4% → 19%
```

### Event 4 — Carrier deterioration

Carrier X:

```text
On-time delivery: 94% → 71%
```

### Event 5 — Invoice lag

One business unit starts invoicing:

```text
3 days after delivery → 11 days after delivery
```

### Event 6 — Customer payment slowdown

A major customer changes from:

```text
Average payment: 35 days → 67 days
```

### Event 7 — Pricing leakage

Some invoices have:

```text
Invoice price < contracted price
```

### Event 8 — Partial shipment problem

Orders are being shipped in multiple batches, increasing freight costs.

### Event 9 — Credit hold

A group of customers exceeds credit limits.

### Event 10 — Dispute spike

One customer suddenly has:

```text
Disputes: $200K/month → $1.4M/month
```

### Event 11 — Product shortage

A high-margin SKU experiences stockouts.

### Event 12 — Returns spike

A product category has an abnormal return rate.

### Event 13 — Duplicate billing

A small number of orders generate duplicate invoices.

### Event 14 — Freight leakage

Expedited shipping costs increase dramatically.

### Event 15 — Revenue trapped in O2C

Large amounts of:

```text
Delivered
BUT
Not invoiced
```

This last one makes a **fantastic executive KPI**.
