**Procure-to-Pay (P2P)**  synthetic-data demo because to tell a complete story from 
**supplier selection → purchase request → PO → receipt → invoice → payment**, and then layer in data quality, 
spend analytics, AP efficiency, compliance, and working-capital insights.

design the data specifically so the dashboard has **intentional business problems planted into it**, rather than generating purely random transactions.

## 1. The P2P process to model

end-to-end flow:

**Supplier Master**
→ **Requisition**
→ **Purchase Order**
→ **Goods Receipt / Service Receipt**
→ **Supplier Invoice**
→ **3-Way Match**
→ **Accounts Payable**
→ **Payment**

And add a few cross-cutting datasets:

* Supplier hierarchy / parent companies
* Supplier contracts
* Commodity/category hierarchy
* Employees / buyers / departments
* Cost centers
* GL accounts
* Approval workflow
* Payment terms
* FX / currency
* Risk/compliance attributes

This gives you much more interesting analytical possibilities than just PO + invoice data.

# 3. Most important: preserve the transaction chain

The data shouldn't just be separate tables.

You want relationships like:

```text
Supplier
   │
   ├── Contract
   │
   └── Requisition
          │
          ▼
      PO Header
          │
          ▼
      PO Line
          │
          ▼
      Goods Receipt
          │
          ▼
      Invoice
          │
          ▼
    3-Way Match
          │
          ▼
       Payment
```

For example:

```text
REQ-100234
      ↓
PO-200187
      ↓
Receipt-300442
      ↓
INV-500991
      ↓
MATCH-500991
      ↓
PAY-700812
```

This is extremely important for your demo because you can drill from an executive KPI all the way down to the individual transaction.

# 4. Supplier master — make this interesting

Don't generate 2,000 perfectly clean suppliers.

Plant **supplier master-data problems**.

Example:

| Supplier ID | Supplier Name                   | Normalized Name | Country | Parent | Status |
| ----------- | ------------------------------- | --------------- | ------- | ------ | ------ |
| SUP001      | IBM Corp                        | IBM             | US      | IBM    | Active |
| SUP827      | IBM Corporation                 | IBM             | US      | IBM    | Active |
| SUP992      | I.B.M. Corp                     | IBM             | US      | IBM    | Active |
| SUP1042     | International Business Machines | IBM             | US      | IBM    | Active |

Now you have a fantastic demo:

> **"How much are we actually spending with IBM?"**

The answer requires supplier normalization / hierarchy.

You can deliberately create:

* Duplicate suppliers
* Duplicate supplier sites
* Different spellings
* Different addresses
* Different tax IDs
* Same supplier under multiple legal entities
* Inactive suppliers still receiving POs
* Suppliers sharing bank accounts
* Suppliers with missing tax information

This can become a **Supplier 360 / Data Quality** story.

# 6. Plant realistic P2P problems

This is the most important part of the synthetic dataset.

I would intentionally plant **15–20 business events**.

### Event 1 — Maverick spend

Some employees bypass procurement.

```text
PO Spend                 $50M
Non-PO Spend              $8M
Maverick Spend             $5M
```

Dashboard question:

> "Which departments have the highest maverick spend?"

---

### Event 2 — Invoice matching problem

Create invoices where:

```text
PO = $100,000
Receipt = $100,000
Invoice = $118,000
```

Result:

**18% invoice variance**

Then ask:

> "What suppliers are generating the most invoice discrepancies?"

---

### Event 3 — Quantity mismatch

```text
PO quantity       1,000
Received            800
Invoiced          1,000
```

This creates a classic 3-way-match exception.

---

### Event 4 — Price mismatch

```text
PO price       $100
Invoice price  $115
```

Create thousands of these, but concentrate them around **10–20 suppliers**.

That allows the dashboard to identify the pattern.

---

### Event 5 — Late receipts

Create suppliers where:

```text
Expected delivery: June 10
Actual receipt:    June 27
```

Then correlate:

**Supplier → Category → PO → Delivery performance**

---

### Event 6 — Slow invoice approval

Plant invoices that sit in approval for:

* 2 days
* 5 days
* 10 days
* 20+ days

Then create one department where invoice approval suddenly deteriorates.

---

### Event 7 — Duplicate invoices

Create:

```text
INV-10021
INV-10021
```

or slightly different invoice numbers:

```text
ABC-12345
ABC12345
ABC-012345
```

Same:

* Supplier
* Amount
* Date
* PO

Now you can demonstrate:

> "Find potentially duplicated invoices."

---

### Event 8 — Early-payment opportunity

Supplier terms:

```text
2% / 10 Net 30
```

But invoices are being paid on day 29.

Dashboard:

> "How much cash discount are we leaving on the table?"

This is a **great CFO story**.

---

### Event 9 — Late payments

Create invoices where:

```text
Due date:     July 10
Paid date:    July 28
```

Then calculate:

**Days Past Due**

and:

**Supplier payment performance**

---

### Event 10 — Supplier concentration

Create a category where:

```text
Supplier A     42%
Supplier B     31%
Supplier C     15%
Others         12%
```

Then ask:

> "Where are we over-dependent on a small number of suppliers?"

# 14. The AI questions I would build around the data

This is where your demo can become much stronger than a traditional BI dashboard.

### Executive questions

> What is driving the increase in procurement spend this quarter?

> Which categories have the greatest savings opportunity?

> Why did invoice exceptions increase this month?

> Which suppliers are responsible for most of our P2P delays?

---

### Procurement questions

> Which suppliers have the highest spend growth but declining delivery performance?

> Find categories where spend increased more than 20% while supplier count decreased.

> Which suppliers have significant non-contract spend?

> Where are we paying different prices for the same product?

---

### AP questions

> What are the top causes of invoice exceptions?

> Which suppliers have the highest duplicate invoice risk?

> Which invoices are overdue and why?

> How much early-payment discount are we missing?

---

### Fraud/risk questions

> Find suppliers sharing the same bank account.

> Find purchase orders just below approval thresholds.

> Identify potential PO splitting.

> Find suppliers with unusual invoice frequency.

> Find employees associated with suspicious supplier relationships.

---

### Advanced cross-domain questions

These are particularly good demos because they require joining multiple tables:

> **Which suppliers have high spend, poor delivery performance, and high invoice exception rates?**

> **Which departments have the highest spend leakage from non-contract purchases?**

> **Which suppliers are increasing prices while their delivery performance is deteriorating?**

> **How much cash could we save if all eligible invoices took the early-payment discount?**

> **Which categories have the greatest opportunity for supplier consolidation?**

# 16. Make the synthetic data intentionally imperfect

I'd use approximately:

| Data condition         | % of data |
| ---------------------- | --------: |
| Normal transactions    |       85% |
| Minor anomalies        |        7% |
| Operational problems   |        5% |
| Fraud/risk scenarios   |        2% |
| Extreme planted events |        1% |

The key is that the anomalies should **cluster**, not be random.

For example:

```text
Supplier ABC
    ↓
Price variance ↑
Invoice exceptions ↑
Delivery delays ↑
Spend ↑ 31%
```

That creates a genuine story:

> **"ABC Technology has become one of our fastest-growing suppliers, but its operational performance is deteriorating."**

That's much better than a dashboard saying "ABC has a high exception rate."

# 18. One additional thing I'd strongly recommend

Since you're building this for an Incorta-style demo, don't make this just a star-schema analytics dataset.

Preserve the **transaction lineage**:

```text
Supplier
   ↓
Requisition
   ↓
PO
   ↓
PO Line
   ↓
Receipt
   ↓
Invoice
   ↓
Match
   ↓
Payment
```

Then you can demonstrate the value of being able to go from:

**$284.6M procurement spend**

all the way to:

**Supplier → PO → PO line → receipt → invoice → payment**

without losing the business context.

That is exactly where a P2P demo can become a powerful **"360° operational analytics + AI"** story.
