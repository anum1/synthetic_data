**HR / Workday-style synthetic data is excellent for a dashboard demo** because you can create a story that moves from executive workforce KPIs → compensation → payroll → benefits → organization → employee-level investigation → AI-driven root-cause analysis.

Design the data around a handful of business events that create interesting questions and visible patterns.

## 1. The demo story 

Imagine your fictional company:

**GlobalTech — 18,500 employees across US, Canada, UK, Germany, India and Japan**

Your dashboard opens with:

> **“Why did our workforce cost increase 8.7% this year while headcount only increased 3.2%?”**

Then the dashboard lets you drill into:

**Workforce**
→ Headcount increased
→ Engineering and Sales drove growth
→ Attrition increased in a few critical organizations

**Compensation**
→ Average salary increased
→ But salary growth isn't evenly distributed
→ Certain job families have compression problems

**Payroll**
→ Payroll cost increased faster than headcount
→ Overtime and bonuses explain part of the increase
→ A payroll anomaly appears in one business unit

**Benefits**
→ Benefits cost per employee increased
→ Healthcare enrollment shifted
→ A new benefit plan caused unexpected cost

**Employee investigation**
→ Drill from company → department → manager → employee
→ See salary, compensation history, payroll, benefits and job history

That gives you a much more compelling demo than simply showing an HR dashboard.

# 13. The synthetic data should contain intentional business events

This is the secret to making the demo good.

Don't generate purely random data.

Plant events.

I'd create at least these:

### Event 1 — Engineering hiring surge

Cloud Engineering hires **450 employees** over six months.

Result:

> Engineering headcount +18%

---

### Event 2 — Sales attrition spike

Sales voluntary attrition jumps from **8% → 17%**.

Mostly among:

* Account Executives
* Sales Engineers
* high performers

---

### Event 3 — Compensation compression

New hires in Engineering are coming in near the salary midpoint while existing employees remain below midpoint.

Question:

> Are we underpaying existing employees?

---

### Event 4 — Promotion wave

Engineering has a large promotion cycle.

Salary expense increases disproportionately.

---

### Event 5 — Benefits inflation

Healthcare employer contribution increases **14%**.

Headcount increases only 3%.

Dashboard detects:

> Benefits cost increased significantly faster than workforce.

---

### Event 6 — Payroll anomaly

One business unit accidentally receives excessive overtime payments for two pay periods.

This creates an obvious anomaly.

---

### Event 7 — Acquisition

GlobalTech acquires a fictional company:

**DataSphere**

~600 employees enter the organization.

Now you have:

```text
Acquired Employees
New Organization
New Locations
New Benefit Plans
Different Salary Structures
```

Fantastic demo story.

---

### Event 8 — Reorganization

Marketing gets reorganized.

Employees move:

```text
Marketing
   ↓
Product Marketing
Demand Generation
Corporate Communications
```

Now your hierarchy/history functionality becomes useful.

---

### Event 9 — High performer attrition

Plant a pattern:

> Employees with performance rating 4–5 and salary below market midpoint have significantly higher resignation rates.

This is **excellent for AI analysis**.

---

### Event 10 — Manager problem

One manager has:

* high turnover
* low engagement
* high absenteeism
* low performance scores

This creates a compelling drill-down.

# 15. The most impressive AI questions

Your synthetic data should be designed so these questions have **real SQL answers**, not just LLM-generated narratives.

### Workforce

> How has headcount changed over the last 12 months?

> Which organizations contributed most to headcount growth?

> What is our voluntary attrition rate by job family?

> Which locations have the highest regrettable attrition?

### Compensation

> Which employees are below 90% of their salary range midpoint?

> Which job families have the greatest salary compression?

> Compare salary growth against performance ratings.

> Are high performers receiving larger salary increases?

### Payroll

> Why did payroll increase this quarter?

> Decompose the payroll increase into headcount, salary, bonus, overtime and commission.

This one is **excellent**.

For example:

```text
Payroll +8.7%

Headcount        +2.9%
Salary increases +3.1%
Bonus            +1.4%
Overtime         +0.9%
Other            +0.4%
```

### Benefits

> Which benefit plans drove the increase in employer benefits cost?

> What is benefits cost per employee by country?

### Management

> Which managers have the highest voluntary attrition?

> Are employees leaving certain managers despite strong performance?

### Workforce planning

> Which departments are growing faster than their budgeted headcount?

> Which job families have the highest hiring demand?

## 19. Architecture I'd use

I'd structure it like this:

```text
                    HR ANALYTICS
                         │
        ┌────────────────┼────────────────┐
        │                │                │
    Workforce       Compensation       Payroll
        │                │                │
    Employee        Salary History     Payroll
    Organization    Job               Bonus
    Position        Performance        Benefits
        │                │                │
        └────────────────┼────────────────┘
                         │
                   AI / SQL Layer
                         │
             ┌───────────┼───────────┐
             │           │           │
          Dashboard    NLQ Chat    Alerts
```

For your use case, I'd keep the **raw Workday-style tables** separate from analytical tables/materialized views. That gives you an opportunity to demonstrate both **enterprise data modeling and fast analytics**.
