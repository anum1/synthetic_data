For a **supply-chain dashboard demo** design the synthetic data **backward from the demo story** so that the dashboard can expose realistic problems, drill into root causes, and let AI answer sophisticated questions.

The goal should be that you can start with:

> **“Our supply chain looks healthy at the aggregate level, but where are we actually at risk?”**

…and progressively uncover inventory, supplier, demand, fulfillment, and cost problems.

## 1. The demo story I recommend

Build the dataset around a fictional global manufacturer/retailer with:

**5 major regions → 15 countries → 40 distribution centers → 200 suppliers → 1,500 products → ~100,000 customers**

And roughly **3 years of daily/weekly history**, e.g. Jan 2024–Dec 2026.

Your dashboard story can follow this progression:

### Executive view

Show:

* Revenue
* Gross margin
* Inventory value
* Inventory turns
* Fill rate
* OTIF %
* Stockout %
* Backorder value
* Supplier on-time %
* Forecast accuracy
* Working capital
* Supply-chain risk score

Then reveal:

> **Inventory is up 14%, but service level has deteriorated.**

That's a much better demo than simply showing KPIs.

# 9. Create 10–15 planted business events

This is the **secret sauce**.

Don't just generate distributions. Plant specific events that your dashboard/AI can discover.

For example:

### Event 1 — Supplier disruption

Supplier S-104 experiences a capacity problem for 8 weeks.

Impact:

* lead time +35%
* on-time rate ↓
* inventory ↓
* stockouts ↑

---

### Event 2 — Demand spike

Product family "SmartHome" experiences a 28% demand increase.

Cause:

Marketing campaign.

Impact:

* forecast error ↑
* stockouts ↑
* expedited shipments ↑

---

### Event 3 — Excess inventory

A group of slow-moving products gets over-forecast.

Impact:

* inventory +30%
* turns decline
* carrying cost increases

---

### Event 4 — Carrier degradation

Carrier C-07 develops chronic delays in the Northeast.

Impact:

* delivery time +2.5 days
* OTIF declines
* freight cost increases

---

### Event 5 — Quality problem

Supplier S-137's defect rate increases from 1.5% → 7%.

Impact:

* rejected receipts
* production delays
* customer backorders

---

### Event 6 — Port disruption

Products originating from Asia experience a temporary transportation disruption.

Impact:

* inbound lead time ↑
* inventory ↓
* expedited freight ↑

---

### Event 7 — Forecast model problem

One product category gets systematically under-forecast.

Impact:

* forecast bias
* stockouts
* emergency POs

---

### Event 8 — Safety-stock policy change

Planner increases safety stock by 25% for several products.

Impact:

* inventory ↑
* stockouts ↓ slightly
* working capital ↑ substantially

This gives you a great **"Was the decision worth it?"** demo.

---

### Event 9 — Product substitution

Customers shift from Product A → Product B.

This creates:

* excess inventory of A
* shortage of B

Excellent AI analysis scenario.

---

### Event 10 — Single-source risk

A critical product relies on one supplier.

Then that supplier experiences disruption.

Now you can ask:

> Which products are most exposed to single-supplier risk?

# 12. The killer demo flow

I'd actually script your demo around this sequence:

### Step 1

Open Executive Control Tower.

Say:

> "At first glance, the supply chain looks reasonably healthy."

Then show:

**Revenue ↑**

**Inventory ↑**

But:

**OTIF ↓**

**Stockouts ↑**

---

### Step 2

Ask AI:

> **Why are stockouts increasing even though inventory is up 14%?**

AI should discover:

* inventory concentrated in slow movers
* demand increased in several high-volume SKUs
* safety stock is poorly allocated
* supplier delays affecting critical SKUs

---

### Step 3

Ask:

> **Which products are creating the largest revenue-at-risk exposure?**

Return:

| Product   | Revenue at Risk | Stockout Probability |
| --------- | --------------: | -------------------: |
| Product A |           $4.2M |                  82% |
| Product B |           $3.7M |                  76% |
| Product C |           $2.9M |                  69% |

---

### Step 4

Drill into Product A.

AI discovers:

**Supplier S-104**

↓

Lead time increased from **18 → 29 days**

↓

OTIF dropped from **95% → 71%**

↓

No alternate supplier

↓

Product A inventory falling

---

### Step 5

Ask:

> **What other products are exposed to Supplier S-104?**

Now show dependency analysis.

This is much more impressive than a normal BI dashboard.

---

### Step 6

Ask:

> **If Supplier S-104 remains disrupted for another 30 days, what revenue is at risk?**

Now you have a scenario analysis.

# 14. Make the synthetic data intentionally "messy"

For an enterprise demo, don't make everything perfectly clean.

Include:

* duplicate supplier names
* slightly different supplier names
* missing lead times
* inconsistent product descriptions
* delayed purchase orders
* cancelled orders
* partial receipts
* backorders
* products with no alternate supplier
* unusual spikes
* seasonal demand
* supplier performance degradation
* changing product costs

For example:

```text
Acme Components Inc.
ACME Components
Acme Component Inc
ACME COMP.
Acme Components LLC
```

All should map to:

```text
supplier_master_id = SUP-104
```

This gives you an opportunity to demonstrate **data quality + supply-chain analytics** in the same demo.

## Recommended architecture

I'd ultimately create something like:

```text
                    SUPPLY CHAIN CONTROL TOWER
                              │
          ┌───────────────────┼───────────────────┐
          │                   │                   │
       Demand             Supply              Logistics
          │                   │                   │
     Forecast            Suppliers             Carriers
     Sales               POs                   Shipments
     Customers           Quality               Transit
          │                   │                   │
          └───────────────────┼───────────────────┘
                              │
                         INVENTORY
                              │
                    ┌─────────┴─────────┐
                    │                   │
                 Products           Warehouses
                    │                   │
                    └─────────┬─────────┘
                              │
                       FINANCIAL IMPACT
                              │
                 Revenue / Margin / WC / Cost
                              │
                              ▼
                         AI ANALYST
```

### My recommendation for your demo

Don't start by generating the CSVs yet.

**First create the synthetic-data specification** with:

1. **15–20 tables**
2. Exact columns and data types
3. Primary/foreign keys
4. Relationships
5. Row counts
6. Date ranges
7. Dimension hierarchies
8. Realistic distributions
9. Seasonal patterns
10. **15 planted supply-chain events**
11. Cross-table causal relationships
12. KPI definitions
13. 50+ AI questions
14. Expected answers for the planted events
15. SQL queries that should answer the questions

That will give you a dataset where the **dashboard, AI analyst, drilldowns, anomaly detection, and scenario analysis all tell the same coherent story**, rather than looking like unrelated random synthetic data.
